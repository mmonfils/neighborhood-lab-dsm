# Our Approach

This document lays out the core beliefs of our work and shows how they shape what we do every day on our blocks. We keep our language casual because these ideas belong on a front porch, not in a textbook. This approach keeps our focus local, protects our privacy, and helps us build real trust right on the block. 

Our work is built on a solid historical foundation inspired by Maria Pearson from Iowa. As a leader who stood up for human dignity and community strength, she proved that lasting change starts when you take direct action right from where you live. Her example inspires us to confront systems of influence and find real empowerment in our own neighborhood resources.

## The Big Picture Connection
We do not work alone. Our efforts are guided by a custom blueprint, which connects this project to two other sub-projects and the Social Work Sandbox umbrella project. We are linked to 515 Main Street and The Brickyard Framework under the Social Work Sandbox umbrella project. 

Our common anchor for these projects is democratic federalism. We call this our local focus. It means we believe that power belongs at the nearest, most local level possible, right on our residential blocks, while still keeping an eye on how our area fits into the larger picture of the surrounding region.

## 1. Building on What We Have (Local Wealth)

Some outside organizations, programs, and traditional charities look at our neighborhoods and only see problems to fix. They may focus on what is missing, which can lead to bringing in outside experts or top-down funding to manage things.

Building from Maria Pearson's example of grassroots community building, we do the exact opposite. We look at our blocks and see places that are already wealthy. We look at our blocks for what we have, not just what we need. We break this community wealth down into three simple areas:
1. **Tools:** The physical gear, open spaces, and shared hardware sitting in our garages and storage.
2. **Skills:** The useful abilities we have, like cooking, building, translating, fixing cars, or caring for kids.
3. **Knowledge:** Our deep understanding of local history, community resources, and how to navigate systems.

## 2. Absolute Privacy (Security Boundaries)

Real trust might not exist if neighbors feel like they are being watched or tracked by others. Many modern tech tools and community apps rely on cloud systems that collect private data, track locations, and share user details with outside interests.

We protect our neighborhoods by keeping our data strictly in the hands of neighbors. We use simple offline spreadsheets that can be printed out on paper or saved on a regular storage drive. There is no central boss or citywide database tracking everyone. Every block grid manages its own local files independently. If two blocks want to coordinate, they talk to each other directly as equals, neighbor to neighbor. This builds self-reliance and independence from the ground up.

## 3. Seeing the Big Picture (Tracking the Blueprint)

This is our way of looking at the world to see how large-scale forces shape our daily lives. Recent decades have involved decisions made in corporate boardrooms, courts, and legislative halls, and these strategies slowly rewrite the rules of local planning. It suggests an effort being made to shift decision-making power away from communities and into centralized hands.

If you only look at your immediate surroundings, you might miss these larger patterns. By tracking how these trends work, we can spot the changes coming before they reach us. Tracking the blueprint is not about red or blue politics. It is about looking at the structural choices coming through city hall with honesty.

Inspired by Pearson's mindset of assertive community action, the plain facts take priority over keeping score. We pursue that so our neighborhoods can point out changes early and prepare their households. We discuss these trends on our Substack newsletter so everyone can learn to spot them in real time.
