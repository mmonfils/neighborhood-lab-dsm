# The Blueprint Tracker

This template is a simple workbench for neighbors to break down big city hall plans, regional funding shifts, and changing town rules into clear, everyday facts. By tracking how large-scale choices trickle down to central Iowa streets, independent blocks can keep their own local records, work out neighborhood friction early, and give their households a steady buffer against outside pressures.

---

## 1. The Pressure Point
*Look past the official announcements to see what changes are moving through city hall in the most straightforward, practical terms.*

* **The Core Shift:** What exact rule, town law, or official decision is being changed, and what is the public reason given for it?
* **The Street-Level History:** How has this specific issue played out on Des Moines or central Iowa blocks in the past? How did our streets handle this matter before it became professionalized or centralized?
* **The Real Driver:** Moving past political arguments or public debates, what is the core structural engine pushing this change (such as new developer financing, city zoning expansions, or merged town departments)?

---

## 2. The Behind-the-Scenes Map
*Map out the actual forces driving the change, separating the public sales pitch from the shifting of local decision-making power.*

* **The Sales Pitch vs. The Actual Shift:** What is the official story being marketed to the community? In contrast, what practical choice or independent control is being moved away from the neighborhood layout?
* **The Driving Forces:** Who is organizing or funding this change? What centralized groups, out-of-state developers, or official committees are anchoring the effort?
* **The Exposure Map:** Which specific blocks, neighborhoods, or house styles will feel this change first? Which surrounding streets or local networks will experience the secondary ripple effects?

---

## 3. The Everyday Impact
*Translate high-level town plans into tangible, long-term block realities over the next five to ten years.*

* **The Immediate Friction:** What are the direct, visible changes to the physical street layout, property rules, or neighborhood access?
* **The Resource Flow:** How does this decision alter the flow of local land use, neighborhood assets, or household budgets within our block footprint?
* **The Long-Term Grid Shift:** What are the slower, unadvertised alterations to neighborhood rights, duties, or local standing that will show up down the road? How does it affect the everyday independence of a standalone lot or apartment building?

---

## 4. The Porch Shield
*Shift away from asking city hall for new top-down rules. Instead, list the clear facts and independent block tools needed to protect the street.*

* **The Local Records:** What clear, verified data or historical local records do neighbors need to keep in their own files to navigate this change without relying on official gatekeepers?
* **The Block Toolbelt:** What common-sense agreements, independent sharing practices, or everyday communication steps can neighbors use to look out for each other and resolve friction internally?
* **The Offline Tracking Fields:** What specific columns, assets, or tracking points should be added to our local spreadsheets to keep decision-making power right where it belongs—on the front porch?
