# The Front Porch Organizing Playbook

This playbook is a practical step-by-step guide for setting up an independent, self-reliant block network. Inspired by Dr. Ralph Bunche's confidence in our shared capacity to do right, this model bypasses top-down institutional management and places coordination directly back on the front porch where it belongs.

---

### Step 1: Walk Your Block
Before opening a spreadsheet, open a conversation. Spend time walking your street or apartment hallway. Talk with your immediate neighbors over the fence or on the porch to understand the shared interests, immediate needs, and overall mood of the block.

### Step 2: Establish a Low-Barrier Baseline
Set up your initial local coordination file using a clean, offline layout. Real progress happens when we lower barriers to entry so that everyday people—not elite corporate or institutional specialists—can safely run their own networks. 

### Step 3: Set Up Your Offline Spreadsheets
Gather your block’s baseline information locally using our standardized, privacy-first formats. Keep these files on your local home hard drives or printed out on physical paper. We explicitly reject centralized third-party tracking programs to protect household data from outside corporate or institutional eyes.
*   **The Neighborhood Toolkit:** Use `neighborhood-toolkit-template.csv` to map local tools, skills, and knowledge across your block.
*   **The Exchange Log:** Use `exchange-log-template.csv` to keep a simple, transparent ledger of day-to-day borrowing and neighbor-to-neighbor help.

### Step 4: Map Your Resources and Big Picture Pressures
Once the neighborhood network baseline is set, a block remains resilient by expanding its visibility across two separate layers of shared community infrastructure:
*   **Public Resource Mapping:** For shared resources that sit out in the open neighborhood space rather than private backyards, use the `neighborhood-index-template.csv` format found in your index directory. This tracks community wealth items like public fridges, free pantries, or seed libraries so anyone can leverage them safely.
*   **Navigating External Pressures:** To protect your streets against rapid transitions, use `big-picture-framework.md`. This gives independent neighbors a plain-spoken tool to document institutional developments or shifting outside messaging, allowing you to bypass messaging and look out for your blocks early.

### Step 5: Keep the Grid Active and Rotating
Prevent centralized authority or organizing burnout on your block by rotating basic tasks regularly. Use our shared rotation layouts to ensure responsibilities move fluidly across different households, keeping your block grid healthy, fair, and truly self-reliant.
