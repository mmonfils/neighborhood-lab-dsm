# The Front Porch Organizing Playbook

This playbook is a practical step-by-step guide for setting up an independent, self-reliant block network. Inspired by Dr. Ralph Bunche's confidence in our shared capacity to do right, this approach avoids red tape and puts coordination directly back on the front porch where it belongs.

---

### Step 1: Walk Your Block
If there isn't already an ongoing link between the people in your area, then start by opening a conversation. Spend time walking your street or apartment hallway. Talk with your immediate neighbors over the fence or on the porch to understand the shared interests, immediate needs, and character of the block.

### Step 2: Create an Easy Starting Point
Use a simple, offline document to start coordinating neighbors. Agree on an accessible method to communicate such as a group chat over text or Signal. Real progress happens with easy access so that everyday people, not outside experts, can safely run their own networks. 

### Step 3: Set Up Your Offline Spreadsheets
Gather information about your block locally using our standardized, privacy-first formats. Keep these files on your local computer hard drives or printed out on physical paper. We avoid third-party tracking programs to protect household data from outside corporate or institutional interests.
*   **The Neighborhood Toolkit:** Use `neighborhood-toolkit-template.csv` to map local tools, skills, and knowledge across your block.
*   **The Exchange Log:** Use `exchange-log-template.csv` to keep a simple, transparent record of day-to-day assistance that is shared.

### Step 4: Map Your Resources and the Big Picture
Once the neighborhood network is set up, a block stays resilient by looking at the bigger picture in a couple of ways:
*   **Public Resource Mapping:** For shared resources near the neighborhood space that are open to the general public, use `neighborhood-index-template.csv` as your directory. This tracks public resources such as community fridges, free pantries, or libraries so anyone can access them safely.
*   **Navigating the Big Picture:** To protect your streets against external pressures, use `big-picture-framework.md`. This gives independent neighbors a plain tool to track larger trends, developments, and public dialogue, allowing you to connect the dots and watch out for your block.

### Step 5: Keep the Grid Active and Rotating
Prevent centralized authority or burnout on your block by rotating basic tasks regularly. Use our shared rotation layouts to ensure tasks shift between different households, keeping your block grid healthy, fair, and self-reliant.
