# Spreadsheet Blueprint

This guide ensures our community-built files stay simple, accessible, and completely safe for our neighbors to use. When we build spreadsheets for the block, we follow common-sense rules to make sure anyone can open them on an old computer using free, offline software. 

Following the diplomatic and respectful framework of Dr. Ralph Bunche, we prioritize low barriers to entry and maximum transparency. We make tools for everyday people, not elite specialists or corporate databases.

---

## 1. Safety and Privacy Protections

We protect neighbor data with the same care we use to watch over each other's backyards. Our tracking layouts enforce privacy by design:

*   **No Full Names:** Use casual, non-identifying labels like `Dave (Apt 3C)`, `Sarah (Apt 4B)`, or `Marcus (Yellow House)`. 
*   **No Address Columns:** Never log specific street numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, or private financial details.
*   **Keep Data Offline:** Keep all files stored safely on local home hard drives or printed on physical paper logs. We explicitly reject cloud links or centralized tracking programs that could expose household details to outside corporate or institutional organizations.

---

## 2. Core Column Frameworks

To keep files interchangeable across different block grids, use these standardized header titles exactly as written.

### The Neighborhood Toolkit Template (`neighborhood-toolkit-template.csv`)
This layout maps out what our blocks already have, focusing entirely on community wealth:
1.  `Household Name (Location)` - A casual identifier.
2.  `Resource Type` - Categorized strictly as `Tools`, `Skills`, or `Knowledge`.
3.  `Description` - Clear details about what is available (e.g., *10-foot aluminum ladder* or *Fluent Spanish translation*).
4.  `Availability Notes` - Simple parameters (e.g., *Weekends only* or *Advance notice preferred*).
5.  `Contact Method` - How to touch base (e.g., *Knock on side door* or *Leave a note*).
6.  `Last Checked` - The calendar date this entry was verified during a seasonal check-in.

### The Exchange Log Template (`exchange-log-template.csv`)
This decentralized mutual aid ledger tracks simple day-to-day coordination without creating an institutional gatekeeper:
1.  `Date` - The day the interaction happened.
2.  `Household Giving` - The household sharing a tool, pitching in with a skill, or offering knowledge.
3.  `Household Receiving` - The household being supported by their neighbor.
4.  `Resource Shared` - A quick, plain-spoken description of the help (e.g., *Lent chainsaw and safety gear* or *Two hours of welding help on broken porch gate*).
5.  `Notes` - Simple context or status updates (e.g., *Returned clean and gassed up* or *Sat on porch and filled them out together*).

### The Neighborhood Index Template (`neighborhood-index-template.csv`)
This layout catalogs open, shared community resources available to the entire neighborhood space, focusing on physical infrastructure rather than individual households:
1.  `Resource Name` - The clear title of the public asset (e.g., *DMPL Community Fridge* or *DMPL Seed Library*).
2.  `Resource Type` - The structural category of the asset (e.g., *Food Pantry* or *Community Garden Resource*).
3.  `Location` - The specific street address, library branch, or intersection where it is hosted (e.g., *900 Locust St (Central Library)* or *5000 Franklin Ave (Franklin Avenue Library)*).
4.  `Availability Notes` - Access rules or seasonal parameters (e.g., *Accessible during regular library operating hours* or *Available during spring and summer seasons*).
5.  `Last Verified` - The exact date the resource's existence and operational status were confirmed by a local coordinator.
