# Contributing to Neighborhood Lab DSM

We are glad you are looking to help build our shared block infrastructure. Following the diplomatic and respectful framework of Dr. Ralph Bunche, we believe real progress happens when we lower barriers to entry so that everyday people, not elite corporate or institutional specialists, can safely coordinate their own blocks.

---

## Ways to Pitch In

### 1. Improve the Local Templates
If you have ideas for making our core offline spreadsheets simpler or easier to read on old computers, we welcome updates to:
*   `front-porch-manual/neighborhood-toolkit-template.csv`
*   `front-porch-manual/exchange-log-template.csv`
*   `neighborhood-index/neighborhood-index-template.csv`

### 2. Refine the Big Picture Framework
Help us simplify how we dissect outside pressures. If you notice new institutional messaging trends or want to add plain-spoken prompts to `tracking-the-blueprint/big-picture-framework.md`, your insights help neighbors protect their blocks early.

### 3. Share Your Backyard Stories
We do not use GitHub for discussions or complex feature requests. If you have practical organizing tips, local success stories, or lessons learned from running a block grid on your street, we share those directly with the wider community via our [Substack newsletter](https://neighborhoodlabdsm.substack.com/).

---

## Ground Rules for Changes

*   **Keep It Community-Casual:** Avoid academic jargon, corporate buzzwords, and sterile database conventions. Write like you are talking to a neighbor over the backyard fence.
*   **Enforce Privacy by Design:** Never submit modifications that require centralized cloud tracking, third-party software accounts, or the collection of private household data (like full names, exact addresses, or phone numbers).
*   **Stay Grounded:** We make low-tech, highly transparent tools. Keep files compatible with basic, offline software so that any independent organizer can fork or reproduce them easily.
