# The Street Guide 🧭

This is our street-level playbook. It applies Occam’s razor to neighborhood organizing, cutting through bureaucratic red tape to help you build a self-governing, connected mini-republic right outside your door. 

---

## 🗺️ Finding Your Block's Boundaries

We don’t rely on city hall zoning maps or real estate redlining boundaries to tell us who our neighbors are. We map our network by immediate physical proximity and relationship.

* **The Footprint:** A standard block is defined as both sides of your immediate street, spanning from one cross-street intersection to the next (roughly 20 to 40 households).
* **The Backyard Fence:** If your street has deep alleyways or connected backyards where kids play and neighbors talk, look at that immediate cluster as your natural boundary.
* **The Walking Radius:** If a neighbor can look out their kitchen window and see your front porch, they are on your block. Keep it tight so face-to-face communication stays simple.

---

## 🪵 Step-by-Step Block Organizing Protocol

Building a resilient block network doesn't require launching a formal non-profit or filing city permits. It happens by reclaiming the simple tradition of sharing space.

* **Step 1: The Porch Invite**
    * Drop a brief, handwritten note in 10 to 15 neighboring mailboxes.
    * Invite them to sit on your front porch or gather at the end of a driveway for an hour on a weekend afternoon. 
    * Keep the pitch casual: *"Let's get to know who is on our street and figure out how we can look out for each other this winter."*
* **Step 2: The Asset Huddle**
    * Pass around a clipboard during your first gathering. 
    * Ask neighbors to write down what practical tools they own or what skills they enjoy sharing (e.g., snowblower, jumper cables, backup generator, garden tilling, emergency childcare, or checking on seniors).
    * Focus entirely on existing strengths—never framework this around what the street "needs" or lacks.
* **Step 3: Setting the Flat Channels**
    * Decide on a simple, zero-cost communication method that everyone can access without a steep learning curve.
    * Use a basic group text string, a simple email chain, or a physical phone tree printed on a single sheet of paper.
    * Avoid corporate neighborhood apps that sell user data, feature tracking algorithms, or encourage hyper-vigilant surveillance and policing.

## 🛡️ Protecting Your Street From Institutional Capture

A block network is a sovereign, self-reliant collective of neighbors. To keep our local democracy safe, flat, and protected from outside exploitation, we enforce three strict rules:

* **The Zero-Dollar Boundary:** No money changes hands inside the block network, and no monetary valuations are ever placed on tools or favors. This keeps Wall Street and commercial market pressures away from backyard relationships.
* **The No-Jargon Rule:** Keep all manuals, logs, and meeting discussions free of academic social work jargon, clinical assessments, or heavy political theories. Speak like neighbors chatting over a backyard fence.
* **The Independent Engine:** Never hand over control of your asset inventories or communication logs to city agencies, county programs, or external non-profits. If outside institutions want to help, they can support our initiatives as equals, but they do not manage our rosters.
