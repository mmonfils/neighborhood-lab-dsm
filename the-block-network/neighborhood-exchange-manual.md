# The Neighborhood Exchange Manual
*A Non-Monetary System for Neighborhood Reciprocity*

## 1. Core Philosophy: The Power of the Hour
The Neighborhood Exchange is based on the **TimeBanking** model developed by Dr. Edgar Cahn. It operates on a simple, evidence-based principle: **One hour equals one credit.** **Equal Value:** An hour of yard work is worth the same as an hour of legal advice or history research.
* **Asset-Based:** Everyone has something to contribute (Asset-Based Community Development).
* **Non-Market:** This system does not replace the economy; it strengthens the "social glue" that markets cannot provide.

## 2. The Five Rules of Exchange
To keep the system stable and fair, all participants agree to these five standards:
1. **Reciprocity:** We "pay it forward." When you receive support through the exchange, you commit to providing a turn for someone else in the future.
2. **No Cash:** No money ever changes hands. This is a "tax-free" exchange of neighborly service.
3. **Self-Reporting:** Neighbors track their own "deposits" and "withdrawals" using **The Exchange Log** built into their local block records.
4. **Respect:** We value the time of the person providing the help as much as our own.
5. **Safety First:** Neighbors are encouraged to trade in pairs or public spaces (like local coffee shops) until trust is established.

## 3. How to Start an Exchange
* **Step 1: Identify a Need.** (e.g., "I need someone to help me move a couch" or "I need a 20-minute tutorial on using GitHub.")
* **Step 2: Check the Index.** Consult your block's copy of **The Block Toolkit** to find neighbors with a matching skill.
* **Step 3: Make the Ask.** Reach out and propose the trade.
* **Step 4: Execute & Log.** Once the exchange is done, log the hours in your block's exchange log.

## 4. Understanding The Block Toolkit (`the-block-toolkit.csv`)
When setting up a new block registry using our standardized CSV template, use this reference guide to fill out and understand the columns:

* **name:** The first name of the neighbor. This column serves as the title label for the map marker when uploaded to digital tools.
* **Block_ID:** A simple hyper-local tracking code (e.g., DSM-01) to group neighbors on the same immediate street section together.
* **Street_Name:** The street location for immediate geographic reference.
* **Contact_Method:** Reflects how a neighbor prefers to be reached (e.g., Text, Email, or a paper flyer under the door).
* **Strengths_and_Skills:** Focuses strictly on asset-based community development—what neighbors are good at or passionate about sharing.
* **Tools_to_Share:** Physical assets available for a hyper-local lending library (e.g., ladders, lawnmowers, tools).
* **Spaces_Available:** Spaces modeling democratic federalism at the household level (e.g., utilizing a front porch, driveway, or patio as a temporary hyper-local public square).
* **Exchange_Status:** Shows if the neighbor is currently actively trading turns or taking a temporary break.
* **latitude & longitude:** The exact coordinates used by map viewers to place the pin.

## 5. Mapping Assets with uMap (The Placeholder Approach)
To make your street's inventory easy to view, **The Block Toolkit** is fully compatible with **uMap** (an open-source map layer builder built on OpenStreetMap). 

Because looking up precise GPS coordinates can be difficult for everyday neighbors, the template utilizes a **placeholder approach**:
1. **Keep the Defaults:** When adding a new neighbor to the spreadsheet, leave the preset numbers in the `latitude` and `longitude` columns as they are, or duplicate the coordinates from the line above. 
2. **Import the File:** Create a new map on uMap, click the **Import Data** button (the arrow icon on the right sidebar), choose your `the-block-toolkit.csv` file, select **csv** as the format, and upload it.
3. **Drag-and-Drop to Adjust:** All your neighborhood pins will initially land on a single default spot in central Des Moines. Click the **Edit** (pencil) icon on uMap, select a pin, and simply drag it directly onto the correct house or property on your street. uMap will automatically update the coordinates on the backend.
4. **Save and Share:** Save your map layer. You can embed it back into your local chat or keep it as a visual index for the block network.

## 6. Setting Up Communication Channels
To keep participation radically accessible and independent of algorithmic corporate platforms, **The Block Network** does not use third-party neighborhood forums. Instead, each individual block coordinates through flexible channels based on the preferences of its residents:

* **Primary Recommendation (SMS Group Texting):** For maximum inclusion and the lowest barrier to entry, standard text message chains or simple tools like GroupMe ensure every neighbor with a basic cell phone can participate instantly.
* **Dedicated Messaging App (WhatsApp):** A highly accessible middle ground for blocks that prefer dedicated group channels, multimedia sharing, and organized threads without forcing residents to manage unfamiliar software.
* **Privacy-Focused Option (Signal):** For blocks that collectively prioritize open-source security, data minimization, and corporate independence, Signal Private Messenger is our preferred platform for secure local coordination.

## 7. Example "Assets" to Trade
* **Physical:** Gardening, snow shoveling, grocery runs, minor home repairs.
* **Knowledge:** Tutoring, local history tours, tech support, policy research.
* **Social:** Check-ins for seniors, pet sitting, neighborhood walk-alongs.

By participating, we are building **Social Capital**. This reduces "resource deserts" and creates a resilient community that can advocate for itself in local policy and urban planning conversations.
